# Real need: grocery cost for adults 50+ (Oct 2026)

## Why it is a real need (data)
- AARP Dec 2025 survey (via search summary; page itself was blocked):
  more than 4 in 10 adults 50+ say grocery prices cost more than they can
  afford; 78% concerned; 48% say rising costs changed how they feed their
  household. About 3 in 10 adults 50-64 cut food/utilities/medication due to
  healthcare costs.
- APA Stress in America 2025: money is a significant stressor for two-thirds of
  Americans; 69% of those cite inflation/rising prices.
- Other real needs found: loneliness (40% of 45+, 59% of caregivers 45-64),
  caregiving stress (about three-quarters of caregivers). Not yet researched as
  products.
- 50-64: 42% use AI chatbots, 19% daily (Pew 2026). Half of 50+ distrust
  AI-generated info (AARP 2026).

## Competition (saturated, low price ceiling)
- eMeals: about $4.99/mo, plans built around store sales, hands off to Walmart,
  Kroger, Instacart. 158 BBB complaints, mostly auto-renew and cancel problems.
- Mealime free / $24.99 yr, Eat This Much $8/mo, Samsung Food free, Flipp and
  Basket free, GroceryChop and Recipy (indie, already pitching "AI recipe apps
  do not know what groceries cost").
- Data: Kroger has a free public API; Walmart has no public price API; Instacart
  needs scraping (terms-of-service risk).

## Economics
- ARPU about $5-8/mo. Healthy meal-app churn 5-7%/mo; meal kits 8-15%/mo; 40-60%
  of users lost in 90 days. Consumer-app Meta CPA benchmark about $8.40.
  LTV at 7% churn is roughly $70 gross before app-store fees: thin but not
  impossible. Unproven for this audience.

## Candidate wedge (unproven)
Email/text-first "dinners for two under $X" for 50+ couples on fixed budgets,
priced from their own store, no app to learn, honest billing (eMeals' weak
spot). Test with a concierge MVP: Claude writes the weekly plan, Kroger API
supplies prices, sent by email. Kill if free sample-plan signups are weak.
